Signal-to-noise ratio

The values filled in are an example: a 2 mW signal with 50 µW of noise.

Input parameters:
Signal = signal power, or RMS signal amplitude
Noise = noise power, or RMS noise amplitude, in the same bandwidth (amplitudes measured across the same impedance)
$$ \mathrm{SNR} = \frac{P_s}{P_n} = \left(\frac{A_s}{A_n}\right)^2 $$
$$ \mathrm{SNR_{dB}} = 10\log_{10}\frac{P_s}{P_n} = 20\log_{10}\frac{A_s}{A_n} $$
SNR (power ratio) = —
Amplitude ratio = —
SNR = — dB
Output parameters:
SNR (power ratio) = signal power over noise power
Amplitude ratio = RMS signal amplitude over RMS noise amplitude, the square root of the power ratio
SNR in dB = 10 log₁₀ of the power ratio, which equals 20 log₁₀ of the amplitude ratio

Formula: P. Horowitz and W. Hill, The Art of Electronics, 3rd ed. (Cambridge University Press, 2015).
