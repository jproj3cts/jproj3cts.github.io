Lens maker’s equation

The values filled in are an example: an N-BK7 biconvex lens in air (n = 1.5168 at 587.6 nm), with radii of 100 mm and a 5 mm centre thickness.

Input parameters:
nₗ = lens refractive index
nₘ = refractive index of the medium around the lens
R₁ = radius of the first surface the light meets
R₂ = radius of the second surface
d = centre thickness (0 for a thin lens)
Sign convention: light travels left to right, and a radius is positive when its centre of curvature lies to the right of the surface. A biconvex lens has R₁ > 0 and R₂ < 0. For a flat surface enter a very large radius, such as 1e12.
$$\frac{1}{f} = \frac{n_l-n_m}{n_m}\left[\frac{1}{R_1}-\frac{1}{R_2} + \frac{(n_l-n_m)\,d}{n_l R_1 R_2}\right]$$
f = —
$$\text{BFL} = f\left(1 - \frac{(n_l-n_m)\,d}{n_l R_1}\right)$$
BFL = —
$$\text{FFL} = f\left(1 + \frac{(n_l-n_m)\,d}{n_l R_2}\right)$$
FFL = —
$$P = \frac{n_m}{f}\quad (f \text{ in metres})$$
P = —
Output parameters:
f = effective focal length, measured from the principal planes
BFL = back focal length, from the second surface to the back focal point
FFL = front focal length, from the front focal point to the first surface
P = lens power in dioptres
Lengths are given in the unit chosen for R₁.

Formula: the thick-lens maker’s equation, E. Hecht, Optics, 5th ed. (Pearson, 2017).
