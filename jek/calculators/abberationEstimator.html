Spherical aberration of a lens

The values filled in are an example: a 100 mm N-BK7 singlet (V = 64.17) at f/4, in yellow light at 587.6 nm.

Input parameters
f = focal length
N = f-number, focal length over aperture diameter
V = Abbe number of the lens glass (dispersion between the F and C lines)
λ = wavelength, for the diffraction-limited spot
$$ d_{\mathrm{sph}} \approx \frac{0.067\,f}{N^3} $$
Spherical blur = —
$$ d_{\mathrm{chr}} \approx \frac{f}{2NV} $$
Chromatic blur = —
$$ d_{\mathrm{Airy}} = 2.44\,\lambda N $$
Airy disc = —
Output parameters
dsph = diameter of the smallest spot from third-order spherical aberration
dchr = diameter of the smallest spot from longitudinal colour across the F to C range
dAiry = diameter of the diffraction-limited spot (first dark ring), for comparison
These estimates are for a single thin lens of index about 1.5, bent for least spherical aberration, with the object at infinity. A blur well below the Airy disc means the lens is close to diffraction-limited.

Formula: thin-lens third-order estimates as given in W. J. Smith, Modern Optical Engineering, 4th ed. (McGraw-Hill, 2008).
