    What this models, and what it does not
    
    
      
    Modelled. Electrostatic fields of any set of electrodes, solved on a grid by finite differences and multigrid, in planar, cylindrical or full three-dimensional symmetry, with open, grounded or mirror boundaries; each electrode’s potential solved once and scaled, so voltages change instantly and an RF electrode swings at its frequency and phase. Electrodes as boxes, rods, tubes, aperture plates, cones, spheres and a Paul trap’s hyperbolic ring and endcaps, or as the bodies of a STEP or STL file. Ions and electrons of any mass and charge, relativistic, flown by an adaptive Runge–Kutta step in the electric and a uniform magnetic field, with hard-sphere collisions on a background gas, Coulomb repulsion between the flying particles (space charge), splats on electrodes, detector planes recording position, time, energy and direction, and a trap analysis from the field’s curvature: Mathieu a and q, secular and Penning frequencies.
    
      
    Not modelled. Magnetic fields other than a uniform one; induced charges and image forces on the electrodes; field emission, secondary electrons and surface physics; dielectrics; a gas that moves or is heated by the beam; the electrodes’ own fringing beyond the array (the array’s boundary stands for it). The grid’s staircase on a curved electrode makes a field there a few per cent off at a coarse spacing: make the spacing finer where it matters, and check a number against an established package before anything is built or published.
