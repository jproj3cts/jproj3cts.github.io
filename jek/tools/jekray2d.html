Import a material

  1. Find the material on refractiveindex.info and choose a data set that covers your wavelengths.
  2. Under the graph, use Data [CSV - nk] (or [CSV - n]) to download the file, or select the numbers and copy them.
  3. Drop the file below or paste the numbers. Wavelengths are in micrometres, which is what that site exports.

Tolerance study

Perturbs the swept parameters at random and traces the bench many times, to see how much the result moves. Each sweep’s From–To range is its tolerance range.

Optimise one value

Searches a swept parameter over its From–To range for the setting that gives the best result. Give the value a sweep first; its range is the search range.

What this models, and what it does not

Modelled. Exact tracing of conic and aspheric surfaces with real glass dispersion; reflection, refraction and total internal reflection; Fresnel coefficients and thin-film coatings with phase; polarisation as complex s and p amplitudes, with waveplates and polarisers; optical path, so wavefront error, interference and cavity round trips; Gaussian beam propagation along the axis, including every branch at splitters and round trips in cavities; physical optics propagation of the beam's diffracted field through the system, with each surface a ray-traced phase screen, as a round field or, where tilts and decentres break the symmetry, a full 2D one that carries the polarisation at every point (each sample through its own Fresnel, coating or waveplate matrix), with branches of one beam adding as fields, phases and polarisation included, wherever they meet on the bench or on a screen; stops (iris, slit, double slit, slit comb, knife edge or beam block, anywhere across the beam) and mask elements with any amplitude and delay you write as a formula, acting on the field and on the rays; and, zoomed in below 50 µm, a close-up that samples the field on every pixel, so standing waves and crossing-beam fringes show, with the fields added as vectors; and at each screen the field that arrives, from Gaussian beams and from ray fans taken as uniform pupils, giving its irradiance map, point spread, Strehl ratio, MTF and enclosed energy. Also diffraction gratings, each order a branch of its own; birefringent crystals (beam displacers with walk-off, Wollaston and Glan–Taylor prisms), where s and p go their own ways; Faraday rotators and isolators, non-reciprocal as they should be; filters and dichroics; cylindrical lenses and axicons; a gain medium, so a cavity shows whether it is above threshold; and fibres and photodiodes, which take in the arriving field as a real one would and report coupled power, photocurrent, output voltage and signal to noise. Sources can have a line of their own (a coherence length, Lorentzian or Gaussian), so fringes fade as two paths grow apart and a cavity builds up the average over the line, and a beam quality M² above 1, carried as a sum of Hermite–Gauss modes added as powers, as from a multimode laser or an LED. The diffracted field (on the bench, at each screen, in a cavity and in the close-up) is worked out in a background thread, so the page stays responsive while it runs.

Not modelled. Anything outside the plane: no skew rays, no out-of-plane tilts, no field analysis in two dimensions, no anamorphic systems. Also no scatter, no thermal effects, no coating dispersion, and no gain saturation: a gain medium sets the threshold, not the power a laser settles at. Power figures ignore absorption unless you set it on a material.

How far to trust it. For anything that gets built, funded or published, reproduce the number in an established package first.

Import from a Zemax file

  1. Open the Thorlabs product page for the lens, mirror, beam splitter or prism and find its part number in the table.
  2. Click the document icon next to the part number and download the .zmx file. If it arrives as a .zip, extract it first.
  3. Choose the file below, or drop it straight onto the bench.

The part is placed at the centre of the view with light travelling left to right, as in the file. Lenses and mirrors come across with their exact surfaces. The file describes cubes and prisms with tilted, folded surfaces that can't be read back reliably, so for those the importer takes the glass and size and builds the part itself. Pick the type above if it guesses wrong.

Glass the tracer doesn't recognise uses the nd and Vd values from the file, which is less accurate far from the visible.
