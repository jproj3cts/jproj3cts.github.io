Lens design
One bench

Simulate the whole bench

Free-space optics, fibre and the electronics after the detector, with photonic chips too, solved together on one bench across as many tabs as you like. Light leaves a fibre and enters free space, lands on a photodiode and carries on as a signal through amplifiers, mixers and lock-ins to an oscilloscope.

Waves, not just rays

Rays, Gaussian beams and the full diffracted field

Exact rays through real glass, each carrying its polarisation and optical path. Gaussian beams followed through every branch, so interferometers show fringes and cavities resonate. And the diffracted field where rays cannot tell the story: slits, masks, a tight focus, a vortex beam. Raise, tip and roll any part and the light follows in 3D, the field glowing along each beam in calibrated W/cm².

Lens design

Design lenses from scratch

From a singlet to a multi-element assembly: build the prescription with solves and pickups, aspheres, freeforms, diffractive surfaces and graded glass, and zooms as configurations. Optimise for a merit function you build, search for new starting points, and choose from some 1,200 glasses in the makers’ catalogues. Then look at it from every side: spots, fans, wavefront, PSF and MTF, relative illumination, ghosts, polarisation, temperature, and a picture as the lens would form it.

How lens design works

To the workshop

From design to drawing to CAD

Tolerance it with sensitivity, Monte Carlo and inverse sensitivity, with compensators and a yield against your specification. Draw each lens in the manner of ISO 10110 and the whole design as a report. Export a .zmx file, or STEP solids and an STL mesh for the mechanical design. And bring your own stock lens files to find which could stand in for each lens, and how well.

The physics

Everything else it models

Beyond the rays and the field, each part carries the physics that makes it work, so a bench behaves as it would in the lab.

Lasers and cavities

  • Gain media, laser threshold and the best output coupler
  • Cavity modes, finesse and linewidth, misaligned cavities included
  • A cavity finding its own mode, as Fox and Li found it
  • Line widths and coherence length: fringes that fade as they should

Pulses

  • Ultrafast pulses through glass, gratings and compressors
  • Dispersion and chirp, pulse by pulse
  • Self-phase modulation and solitons in fibre

Nonlinear and active optics

  • Frequency doubling in PPLN and PPKTP, by Boyd and Kleinman’s theory
  • EOMs and AOMs with their sidebands and frequency shifts
  • Fibre amplifiers and semiconductor optical amplifiers

Atoms and traps

  • Rubidium, caesium and potassium vapour cells: hyperfine lines, Doppler broadening, saturated absorption and the Zeeman effect
  • Optical forces on atoms and particles: tweezers, dipole traps and lattices
  • Levitated nanoparticles, with their trap frequencies and damping

Polarisation and shaping

  • Waveplates, polarisers, Wollaston prisms and birefringent crystals
  • Faraday rotators and isolators
  • Masks, spatial light modulators and vortex beams
  • Vector fields at a tight focus

Signals and workflow

  • Photodiodes, transimpedance amplifiers, mixers, lock-ins, oscilloscopes and spectrum analysers, with their noise
  • Pound–Drever–Hall and other lock error signals
  • Sweep any setting and watch the bench change
  • Mounts from CAD as STL, part packs and your own models as mods
RF and millimetre waves

Quasi-optics from 1 GHz to 3 THz

Feed horns, RF lenses, mirrors, wire grids and mesh filters, with the beam’s diffraction worked out properly at these wavelengths. Plastic lenses made to order, solid or graded in density, show their loss, how well the beam fits them, and whether they are manufacturable. Sweep a link across frequency for its S21 and link budget, steer the beam off axis, and take the signal on to a mixer and a spectrum analyser.

Some things to ask for:

  • “Design a Michelson interferometer at 1064 nm with 100 mm arms and a photodiode on the output. Give me a .jekray file.”
  • “Rebuild the optical setup in figure 1 of the attached paper as a .jekray file. Put your assumptions in the notes.”
  • “Add a mode-matching lens pair so this laser matches the cavity in the file I attached.”

Paste the answer into JEKray with Open ▾ → Pasted text (or Ctrl+V on the bench). If anything could not be used as written, the tool lists it: copy that report back to the assistant and it can fix the file. Check what it built against your source before relying on it.

JEKrayPro Terms Privacy Policy Security overview Accessibility statement JEKrayPro for universities

JEKray works in the plane of the bench. It has no skew rays, no out-of-plane tilts and no anamorphic systems, and it does not model scatter, thermal effects or coating dispersion. Power figures ignore absorption unless you set it on a material.

The documentation sets out each model, its equations and its limits, element by element.

The validation page runs JEKray against published results and independent codes, live in your browser. For anything that gets built, funded or published, check the numbers in an established optical design package first.
