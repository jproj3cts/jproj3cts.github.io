JEKrayPro security overview

Version 1.4, 18 October 2026

How JEKray and JEKrayPro protect users and their data, for information security teams reviewing them. It answers most of a typical supplier security questionnaire; we are glad to complete yours as well. Questions: support@jeksys.net.

1. What it is

  • The simulator is a web page served from jeksys.net, with nothing to install. A bench leaves the user’s device only when they save it to the cloud, share it or export it.
  • The cloud service (api.jeksys.net) holds accounts, benches and their history. It runs on established cloud infrastructure; we operate no servers of our own.
  • The website holds no account data.
  • Payments go through Stripe’s hosted Checkout, customer portal and invoices. Card numbers never reach our systems.
  • The cloud service is separate from our other systems: it shares no data, storage or credentials with them.

2. Sign-in and sessions

  • Users sign in with a Google or Microsoft account, or with an email address proved by a code sent to it, with a password of their own if they choose one. From Google and Microsoft we ask only for the basic sign-in details, and we read nothing from a university’s directory.
  • Microsoft sign-in accepts university and work accounts only. A person’s email address is taken only from an identity their organisation has verified, never from a field the user can edit.
  • Passwords are stored in a form that cannot be reversed, protected by a secret kept outside the database. Wrong guesses are limited and then locked out for a time. A forgotten password is replaced by proving the address again, which ends every existing session.
  • Accounts are never merged because two sign-ins share an email address. A second sign-in method can only be linked by someone already signed in.
  • A session is bound to the browser that started it, cannot be read by scripts or by other websites, and would not let anyone sign in from a copy of our database. Sessions last 30 days from last use and end on sign-out, on a password change, or on account deletion.

3. Access control

  • Each user’s work is private to them and the teams they share it with. Every request is checked against the user’s membership and role in the workspace concerned (owner, admin, editor or viewer). Another workspace’s benches are indistinguishable from ones that do not exist.
  • Only jeksys.net can act for a signed-in user: changes from other websites are refused, and other websites cannot read the service’s responses.
  • Saving needs an active plan; reading and exporting never do, so people can always get their data out.
  • Requests are rate limited, per network address for sign-in and per person for changes and slower actions, and every request is capped in size.
  • A university’s licence administrators see only counts, and can invite or take off people by email address; taking someone off answers the same whether or not they have an account.
  • Our own administration of university licences uses separate credentials over HTTPS, and is switched off entirely when they are not set.

4. Data protection in transit and at rest

  • Data is encrypted in transit and at rest: all traffic to the website and the service is over HTTPS, and stored data is encrypted by our infrastructure provider.
  • API keys and other secrets are held encrypted, never in our source code. Our Stripe key has only the permissions the service needs.
  • The app can load code from, and send data to, jeksys.net and the service only. Benches, including ones opened from share links, are only ever treated as data, never run; the formulas in masks allow arithmetic and named maths functions only.
  • Uploaded data is checked: a bench must be a valid bench file of at most 2 MB, and a thumbnail must really be an image of at most 128 KB, and is served back so that browsers cannot treat it as anything else.
  • Payment events are accepted only when Stripe’s signature on them is valid and recent, each is processed once, and the subscription’s state is always fetched from Stripe rather than trusted from the message.

5. Backups, history and deletion

  • Every bench keeps a version history: the last 50 saved versions, and one a day for 90 days. Users can restore any of them.
  • Deleted benches stay in a bin for 30 days, then are deleted with their history.
  • The service’s data can be restored to an earlier point in time.
  • Users can export all their benches at any time and delete their account. Account deletion removes their workspace, benches, history, sign-ins, password and sessions at once.

6. How changes are made

  • All code is kept in version control, with the history of every change.
  • The service has an automated test suite, including tests that one account cannot reach another’s data, that changes from other websites are refused, and that billing and deletion behave correctly. The simulator is checked by some 870 automated checks against closed-form results and published data. Both are run before changes are released.
  • Before the first users, the whole service and app were reviewed for security, including a test that calls every part of the service naming another person’s data as someone else, signed out, and from another website, and checks that nothing is returned or changed. The service runs no third-party code.

7. People and administration

  • JEK Systems is a small business. Administrative access to the hosting, payment and code accounts is held by its director only, and each of those accounts is protected by two-factor authentication.
  • We do not look at users’ benches, except when a user asks us to help with one.

8. Incidents

  • If we find a security incident affecting personal data, we contain it, assess it, and notify the Information Commissioner’s Office within 72 hours where the law requires, and affected users.
  • Universities with a licence are told without undue delay, and we aim to do so within 48 hours of becoming aware (see Data protection for universities).
  • Please report security problems to support@jeksys.net. We will acknowledge reports within two working days and will not take action against good-faith research that avoids harm to users and their data.

9. What we do not have yet

  • An independent penetration test or a formal certification such as Cyber Essentials or ISO 27001. We will share results when we have them.
  • Sign-in through the UK Access Federation (SAML). University single sign-on is through Microsoft accounts.
